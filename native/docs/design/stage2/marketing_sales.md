### 2. Gameplay purpose
- Duration: real decision (commit N weeks with a lock vs. ongoing and flexible; cheap trial of a risky channel; a seasonal push, e.g. before a `peak` month, SalesWays.swift:127-136 warns 3 weeks ahead). Today the lock is only the channel's `mw` (0 for most channels), so there is no way to say "run this for 8 weeks and stop" and no commitment cost. Benefits small owner (cash control, no forgotten campaign bleeding money) and group (managers can be told "campaign for 12 weeks"). Moderate value.
- Age targeting: as in the original (a flat +-15% on gain, same answer for every trade) it is a WEAK decision: a dominant option (35-49) exists everywhere. It earns a place only if the trade has a real customer age profile so the choice is non-trivial (students for a bar, 50+ for a pharmacy, 25-49 for a gym) and a wrong aim visibly wastes spend. That requires NEW data for 67 trades. Value: medium for the owner who knows the product, low if every trade defaults to "everyone".

### 3. Interactions (and the owner rule: odds, never fixed numbers)
- Money: `Payments.companyPays(... category "mkt")` weekly (Marketing.swift:160-166); campaigns pause when cash minus planned spend is below the owner's reserve (:132-136). A fixed length means a known total cost = `b x weeks`: show before confirming (DESIGN_RULES). Ending a campaign early is already blocked by `lock`.
- Awareness/demand: gain feeds `co.awareness` (:171) then `awarenessFactor`. Age targeting must stay a multiplier on the gain (or on the fit term), never a customer count. Proposed model (odds only):
  - Add per-trade `ageFit: [4 weights]` (18-24, 25-34, 35-49, 50+, each 0.2..1.5, 1 = average) in data (a new `trade_audience.json`, or `aud_age` key in `market_classes.json`-style table; default flat [1,1,1,1] = old behaviour, so un-profiled trades are unaffected).
  - Channel age reach (data in `marketing_insurance.json` `CH`): e.g. tiktok skews young, local paper/billboard older, google/b2b middle. New key `age: [4]`, default 1.
  - Targeted gain multiplier per channel = `0.7 + 0.5 * targetFit` where `targetFit` = clamp(tradeAge[target] * channelAge[target] normalised, 0, 1.2); not targeting (default "Everyone") = 1.0 for both. So aiming right gives up to about 1.15-1.2 (like the original) and aiming at a group the product does not suit gives about 0.7: the SPEND IS PARTLY WASTED, still a chance not a promise.
  - Capacity: awareness above what the company can serve is already wasted by the existing Demand/Capacity min (customers = min(D, cap), `CapacityModel`), so a product that cannot serve (queue, stock-out) turns extra awareness into lost sales and reviews. Preview must say it: if `lastW.util > 0.9` (as SalesWays.swift:131 does) show "You are near your limit: more awareness will not become more sales." Do not add a second capacity penalty.
  - Reach preview = a qualitative band (e.g. "Slow / Steady / Strong build, may vary") from the same formula with the variance of the channel, plus total cost. No customer count.
- Reputation/reviews: awareness brings customers but a bad product lowers rating (existing); no new coupling. Optional later: use real review ages (`Reviews` `a`) to give the owner "your best-rated group is 35-49" like the original `mktPlan` (O/features/company/marketing_manager_and_store_rescue.js:18-33).
- Managers/delegation: `ManagerWeek.swift:281-284/:373-384` and `ManagerProposals.swift:131` create/raise campaigns without `mktEnd`/target: keep (ongoing, target "Everyone"); a marketing manager (`tm.mkt`) multiplies gain (Marketing.swift:179) unchanged. - Chains: `Chains.swift:388` ranks channels with `channelAffinity * fit * efficiency`; brand marketing is chain-level. Keep chains with target "Everyone" in v1 (no per-store age target) to avoid double counting with shared awareness.
- Events: viral roll (:202-225) uses channel fit, not age; leave.
- Saves/golden tests: `mktEnd` already in the save and read; `mktT` new. Golden/parity tests: with default target (everyone) and no length the weekly maths must be byte-identical; add the multiplier only when `mktT` exists. Exploit risk: switch target weekly to pick the best fit: make the target per CAMPAIGN (stored in the `mkt[ch]` record as `ag`), set at start, locked with `lock`; no company-wide `mktT`, which fixes the original's last-write-wins bug.

### Structure: independent / chain / franchise / holding (SIMULATION_RULES.md section 4)
Existing data read: `chain.rules.mkt` and `off.mkt` (Chains.swift:375-400, brand marketing picks the best 2 channels excluding prime-time, national and high-variance ones: Chains.swift:388-389), franchisor `fr.mkt` (0.02 fund), `fr.brand`, `hasHeadOffice("mkt")`.
- Independent: one outlet gets almost nothing from prime-time (`nationalFit` 0.12) unless the trade is national by nature (webshop, airline ... Marketing.swift:111): the player can buy it and mostly waste it, which is intended.
- Chain: prime-time is a chain-level decision (national footprint = `Competition.chainOutlets`); the campaign belongs to ONE company record in the country. v1: any store can run it and its awareness gain is for that store only (awareness is per company); the chain's shared awareness (existing `shared awareness` in Chains.swift, UNVERIFIED lines) spreads it. A chain-wide "buy prime-time for all stores" rule is DEFER (needs rules.mkt state and cost allocation per store). `chainsWeek` keeps excluding prime-time from automatic brand marketing (already `!isPrimeTime`).
- Franchise: a national brand campaign is the franchisor's job, financed from the unit fund `fr.mkt`; a franchisee cannot buy prime-time unless the agreement allows local marketing (same `localMkt` term as item 1). v1: franchise units not simulated as companies, so the franchisor buys it for its own stores.
- Holding: no effect on unrelated companies; a subsidiary in another country has its own country's media (data-driven `media.prime`), no cross-border spend.
- Rule-conformance: no guaranteed result; high-variance outcome and visible waste; no cap on size of effect (1.7x/0.55x/0.9x remain from the original and are explicit variance, not clamps).
